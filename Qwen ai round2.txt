# 3. System Components in Detail

## A. Order Intent & Risk Gateway

This is the first control point. It decides whether an order is normal, medium-risk, or high-risk before it enters the logistics network.

### Inputs

| Data Point | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Payment mode | COD is 80% of orders and has 20% RTO |
| Customer order history | Repeat customers are lower risk |
| First-time address flag | New/unclear addresses have higher failure |
| Order value | High-value COD has higher refusal risk |
| Product category | Some categories have higher refusal |
| Pincode RTO history | Certain areas have higher failure |
| Address quality score | Poor address increases failure |
| Distance from hub | Farther orders have higher RTO |

### Risk Score Output

| Risk Band | Action |
|---|---|
| Low Risk | Normal processing |
| Medium Risk | WhatsApp address confirmation |
| High Risk | IVR call / token advance request |
| Very High Risk | Prepaid conversion or hold until confirmation |

### Example Rule

If:

```text
COD order
+ first-time address
+ incomplete address
+ high-risk pincode
```

Then:

```text
Trigger WhatsApp confirmation before dispatch.
If no response, trigger IVR call.
If still no confirmation, hold order at destination hub.
```

### Why This Wins

This prevents bad orders from traveling through the full network. The case says forward cost is ₹50 and reverse cost is ₹120. If you stop a bad COD order before shipping, you save both.

## B. Address Intelligence Engine

This solves the case challenge:

> Customer addresses are often unclear and rely on landmarks rather than fixed structure.

### Address Quality Score

Create a score from 0 to 100 based on:

| Feature | Signal |
|---|---|
| House number present | Strong address signal |
| Street name present | Strong address signal |
| Landmark present | Useful but weak signal |
| Pincode valid | Basic validation |
| GPS pin dropped | Strong signal |
| Text address matches GPS pin | Very strong signal |
| Address used successfully before | Very strong signal |
| Address contains vague words | Risk signal |

### Example Vague Words

```text
nearby
behind
opposite
old house
big tree
temple
mosque
school
market
```

These are common in Indian addresses but hard for new riders.

### Actions Based on Address Score

| Address Score | Action |
|---|---|
| High | Normal delivery |
| Medium | Ask customer to confirm landmark |
| Low | Request WhatsApp live location |
| Very Low | Ask customer to choose nearby pickup point |

### Address Correction Flow

```text
Customer places order
        |
        v
Address confidence score calculated
        |
        v
If score low:
        |
        v
Send WhatsApp message:
"Please confirm your delivery location"
        |
        v
Customer shares map pin / landmark / voice note
        |
        v
Address enriched before reaching LMDC
```

### Why This Wins

This directly reduces:
- wrong hub routing,
- rider search time,
- failed attempts,
- fake attempts due to address confusion.

## C. Smart Routing Engine

This solves:

> Orders sometimes land at the wrong delivery hub.

### Problem

If an order is assigned to the wrong Last Mile Delivery Center:
- delivery gets delayed,
- rider cannot complete it easily,
- RTO probability increases,
- parcel may travel back unnecessarily.

### Solution

Use geo-fencing + historical delivery data to assign the correct LMDC.

### Components

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Hub geo-fence map | Defines service area of each LMDC |
| Pin-drop mapping | Matches customer location to correct hub |
| Historical success map | Learns which hub successfully delivered to that area |
| Wrong-hub detector | Flags mismatch at sort center |
| Lateral transfer module | Moves parcel to correct hub without full reverse |

### Wrong-Hub Decision Logic

If:

```text
Customer GPS pin is outside assigned LMDC geo-fence
OR
Historical deliveries from this pin were handled by another LMDC
OR
Address text belongs to another delivery zone
```

Then:

```text
Flag order as wrong-hub risk.
Reassign to nearest correct LMDC.
Use local transfer instead of sending parcel back to origin.
```

### Wrong-Hub Recovery Flow

```text
Parcel scanned at Destination Sort Center
        |
        v
Routing model checks hub assignment
        |
        v
If wrong hub detected:
        |
        v
Transfer parcel laterally to correct LMDC
        |
        v
Avoid full RTO journey
```

### Why This Wins

The case explicitly mentions wrong hub routing. Most teams will ignore this. If you solve it, you show depth.

## D. Rider Intent & Difficulty Engine

This solves:

> Riders are paid the same fixed rate for all successful deliveries, so for certain specific orders riders have lower intent, leading to RTO.

The case also shows:

| Distance from Hub | Share of Orders Returned Undelivered |
|---|---:|
| Nearby, ~2 km | 15% |
| Moderate, ~5 km | 17% |
| Far, ~10 km+ | 22% |

This means distance and difficulty matter.

### Delivery Difficulty Score

Each order should get a difficulty score.

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Distance from hub | Farther = harder |
| Address clarity | Poor = harder |
| Customer contact history | Hard to reach = harder |
| Past failed attempts | Higher difficulty |
| Time of day | Evening/night may be harder |
| Area type | Dense lanes, village, gated society all differ |
| Cash amount | High COD may increase refusal risk |

### Dynamic Incentive Model

Instead of flat payout only:

```text
Base delivery payout
+ Difficulty bonus
+ Hard-zone bonus
+ First-attempt success bonus
```

### Example

| Delivery Type | Base Payout | Additional Bonus |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nearby easy delivery | ₹X | ₹0 |
| 5 km moderate delivery | ₹X | ₹3-5 |
| 10 km+ difficult delivery | ₹X | ₹5-10 |
| First-time address with confirmed pin | ₹X | ₹5 |

### Important Guardrail

Do not reduce rider base earnings.

Use this message in your deck:

> Rider bonuses for difficult orders are funded by the reverse logistics cost saved from preventing RTO. A prevented RTO saves ₹120, so paying ₹5-10 extra for a difficult delivery is highly profitable for the system.

### Why This Wins

This aligns rider behavior with Valmo’s goal. It does not punish riders; it incentivizes them to complete hard deliveries.

## E. Delivery Communication Bridge

This solves:

> Riders and customers often cannot reach each other.

### Problem

A rider may be at the correct address but delivery still fails because:
- customer does not pick up,
- rider does not call,
- network issue,
- language barrier,
- customer is at work,
- customer does not recognize unknown number.

### Solution

Create a communication layer between rider and customer.

### Communication Tools

| Tool | Use Case |
|---|---|
| Masked calling | Protect privacy, enable call tracking |
| WhatsApp message | Send delivery notification |
| Automated IVR call | Confirm customer availability |
| Local language SMS | Improve understanding |
| In-app chat | Reduce dependence on personal calls |
| Delivery time window | Let customer choose availability |

### Communication Flow

```text
Rider is 2 km away
        |
        v
Automatic WhatsApp/SMS:
"Your Meesho order is arriving today"
        |
        v
Customer confirms:
- Available now
- Ask rider to call later
- Leave at neighbor/security
- Reschedule
        |
        v
Rider sees response in app
```

### Call Verification

If rider marks “customer unavailable”:

System checks:

```text
Did rider call?
Did call connect?
How long did rider wait?
Was rider within GPS range?
Did customer receive SMS/WhatsApp?
```

If no real contact happened, flag as suspected fake attempt.

### Why This Wins

Many RTOs are not real logistics failures. They are communication failures.

## F. Delivery Proof & Fake Attempt Detection

This solves:

> Not every shipment marked “delivery attempted” was genuinely attempted.

### Fake Attempt Signals

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rider marked “customer unavailable” | Possible failure |
| No call made | Suspicious |
| Call duration zero | Suspicious |
| GPS far from customer pin | Suspicious |
| Rider spent less than 30 seconds at location | Suspicious |
| Multiple failures by same rider in short time | Suspicious |
| Customer says rider never came | Confirms issue |
| Same address failed repeatedly | Needs audit |

### Fake Attempt Risk Score

Create a score for every failed attempt.

| Risk Level | Action |
|---|---|
| Low | Accept attempt |
| Medium | Send customer confirmation SMS |
| High | Reassign order to another rider |
| Very High | Audit rider/hub |

### Delivery Proof Options

| Proof Type | Use Case |
|---|---|
| GPS location at attempt | Basic proof |
| Dwell time at customer location | Stronger proof |
| OTP from customer | High-value or high-risk orders |
| Photo proof | Select cases |
| Customer confirmation link | Post-attempt verification |

### Customer Confirmation Flow

If rider marks failed attempt:

```text
Send customer message:
"Your delivery was attempted. Did the rider contact you?"
```

Options:

```text
Yes, rider called
No, rider did not contact
I was unavailable
I want reschedule
I want to cancel
```

This closes the feedback loop.

### Why This Wins

This directly addresses one of the hardest case challenges: distinguishing real attempts from fake attempts.

## G. Refusal Rescue Engine

This solves:

> Customers refuse orders for many different reasons. Refused orders travel all the way back while incurring double logistics cost.

This is one of the most important parts of your submission.

### Refusal Reason Taxonomy

When a customer refuses, rider must capture the reason:

| Reason | Intervention |
|---|---|
| No cash right now | Offer UPI payment |
| Changed mind | Offer small discount or reschedule |
| Delivery delayed | Apology + discount/reschedule |
| Family member refused | Reschedule or customer confirmation |
| Product not needed | Local recovery |
| Found cheaper elsewhere | Discount if viable |
| Damaged/missing items | Return with proof |
| Customer not reachable | Pickup point option |

# 4. Proposed Solution for Refused/Undelivered Orders

This is a mandatory Round 2 deliverable.

Your final proposal should be:

> Instead of automatically sending every refused order back to the seller, create a three-layer recovery system: Doorstep Rescue, Hub Hold & Pickup, and Local Liquidation.

---

## Layer 1: Doorstep Rescue

When customer refuses COD at doorstep:

Rider app shows:

```text
Customer is refusing order.
Offer rescue option?
```

### Rescue Options

| Option | When to Use |
|---|---|
| Pay via UPI now | Customer has no cash |
| Small discount | Customer is hesitating |
| Reschedule delivery | Customer is unavailable |
| Convert to pickup point | Customer cannot receive now |

### Doorstep UPI Discount Example

Assume:

```text
Reverse logistics cost = ₹120
```

If customer refuses COD:

Offer:

```text
₹30-₹50 discount if customer pays immediately via UPI
```

Economics:

| Scenario | Cost |
|---|---:|
| Order becomes RTO | ₹120 reverse cost |
| Customer pays UPI with ₹40 discount | ₹40 discount cost |

If the order is saved, Valmo avoids ₹120 reverse cost.

Net benefit:

```text
₹120 saved - ₹40 discount = ₹80 net benefit
```

This is a strong business case.

### Guardrails

To prevent customers from intentionally refusing to get discounts:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Customer repeatedly refuses to get discount | Limit rescue offer to once per customer |
| Chronic refusers abuse system | Flag high-risk customers |
| Riders create fake refusal | Require refusal reason + customer confirmation |

---

## Layer 2: Hub Hold & Pickup Point

If customer is unavailable or refuses due to timing:

Instead of immediate RTO:

```text
Hold parcel at LMDC for 24-48 hours.
```

Then offer customer:

```text
- Redelivery tomorrow
- Pick up from nearby hub
- Pick up from kirana/partner point
- Small pickup discount
```

This is especially useful in:
- gated societies,
- hostels,
- offices,
- villages,
- areas where customers are not available during delivery time.

---

## Layer 3: Local Liquidation Network

If customer refuses and does not respond to rescue options, do not immediately send the parcel back.

Instead:

```text
Move parcel to Local Recovery Pool at LMDC.
```

### Local Recovery Options

| Option | Description |
|---|---|
| Sell to nearby customer | LMDC entrepreneur uses WhatsApp/local network |
| Sell to local kirana partner | Partner sells product offline |
| Open-box sale | Sell at discount with transparency |
| Return pooling | Aggregate returns to reduce reverse cost |
| Seller-approved local resale | Seller gives permission for local recovery |

### LMDC Entrepreneur Incentive

The local delivery hub entrepreneur gets a commission for recovering the order.

Example:

```text
If refused product is sold locally:
- Valmo avoids reverse logistics cost
- Entrepreneur earns commission
- Seller gets faster recovery
- Inventory does not travel back
```

### Local Liquidation Flow

```text
Customer refuses order
        |
        v
Rider captures refusal reason
        |
        v
Doorstep rescue attempted
        |
        v
If failed:
Parcel goes to LMDC recovery pool
        |
        v
Entrepreneur lists product locally
        |
        v
If sold locally:
Order marked locally recovered
        |
        v
If not sold within 48 hours:
Return pooling initiated
```

### Why This Wins

This directly answers:

> What could be done with refused/undelivered orders instead of sending them all the way back?

It is innovative, practical, and financially strong.

# 12. Risk Management Framework

This is also mandatory.

## Risk 1: Customers Abuse Discounts

### Risk

Customers may refuse COD just to get a doorstep discount.

### Mitigation

| Control | Action |
|---|---|
| Customer trust score | Identify repeat refusers |
| Offer limit | One rescue offer per customer |
| Risk-based exclusion | High-risk customers not eligible for discount |
| Refusal reason verification | Customer confirmation after refusal |

---

## Risk 2: Riders Game Difficulty Bonus

### Risk

Riders may claim difficult delivery bonus for normal deliveries.

### Mitigation

| Control | Action |
|---|---|
| GPS validation | Bonus only if route and location match |
| Address confidence | Bonus only for verified difficult addresses |
| Audit sampling | Random manual checks |
| Rider history | Detect repeated bonus claims |

---

## Risk 3: Fake Attempts Increase Due to Pressure

### Risk

If riders are measured too aggressively, they may fake attempts.

### Mitigation

| Control | Action |
|---|---|
| Do not penalize genuine failures immediately | Use audit first |
| Reward first-attempt success | Positive incentive |
| Customer confirmation | External verification |
| GPS dwell proof | Technical proof |

---

## Risk 4: Local Liquidation Creates Inventory Leakage

### Risk

LMDC entrepreneurs may misuse refused inventory.

### Mitigation

| Control | Action |
|---|---|
| Barcode scan | Every recovered item tracked |
| Photo proof | Required for local sale |
| Commission incentive | Entrepreneur earns legally |
| Audit dashboard | Hub-level recovery tracking |

---

## Risk 5: Customer Friction Reduces Orders

### Risk

Too much COD friction may hurt Meesho’s customer experience.

### Mitigation

| Control | Action |
|---|---|
| Risk-based friction only | Trusted customers unaffected |
| WhatsApp-first confirmation | Low friction |
| Token advance instead of full prepaid | Easier for customers |
| A/B testing | Measure conversion impact |

# 15. One-Paragraph Final Architecture Summary

> Our proposed solution is a Valmo Delivery Assurance Platform that combines an ML-powered RTO Control Tower with ground-level operational interventions. The system begins at order placement by scoring COD intent, address quality, and customer risk. It corrects wrong hub routing before last-mile dispatch, improves rider-customer communication, introduces delivery difficulty-based incentives, and verifies delivery attempts using GPS and customer confirmation.
